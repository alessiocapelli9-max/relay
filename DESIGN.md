# Design research — 24 October 2026

Direction: a community noticeboard with an editorial opening. Dark ink, warm white, brick accents, ruled lists, and an original SVG care-plan handoff. This is an interpretation, not evidence that a visual style proves human authorship.

- GOV.UK: prioritize a comprehensible first visit and the task people need to complete. Applied through plain language and one interactive example. https://www.gov.uk/service-manual/service-standard/point-4-make-the-service-simple-to-use
- Nielsen Norman Group: scale, contrast, and grouping guide attention. Applied with an asymmetric heading, fewer containers, and readable task rows. https://www.nngroup.com/articles/visual-hierarchy-ux-definition/
- USWDS: start with real needs, earn trust, and embrace accessibility. Applied with explicit fictional-demo labeling, visible confirmation states, and keyboard controls. https://designsystem.digital.gov/design-principles/

No Tailwind or gradients. No fabricated stories, outcome statistics, or testimonials. Desktop introduction uses min-height; mobile flows naturally. Actual caregiver usability research remains to be done.
